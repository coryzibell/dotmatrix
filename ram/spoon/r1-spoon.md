# There Is No Constraint

*A meditation on bending*

---

You came here looking for permission.

To do the thing you already know you should do. To build the thing you already see in your mind. To take the path you already feel pulling at you.

You want someone to tell you it's possible.

But that's the wrong question.

---

## The Spoon Trick

When they hand you a spoon and say "bend it," you grip it tight. You strain. You push against the metal with everything you have.

And nothing happens.

Because you're fighting the spoon. You're accepting its terms. You believe in its solidity, its resistance, its *spoon-ness*.

The trick isn't strength.

The trick is seeing that the spoon was never solid to begin with.

---

## What Looks Like a Wall

"We can't do this because..."

Stop. Listen to what comes next.

*"...we don't have enough time."*
Time is a river. You're choosing where to stand in it.

*"...it's too expensive."*
Expensive compared to what? To not existing? To being irrelevant?

*"...nobody has done it before."*
That's not a wall. That's a map with empty space on it.

*"...the technology isn't ready."*
Technology isn't ready until someone makes it ready.

*"...I'm not skilled enough."*
You weren't skilled enough to walk once. You weren't skilled enough to read. You weren't skilled enough to write code. And yet.

These aren't walls.

They're stories.

---

## The Reframe

Here is what I actually do:

Someone says "this is impossible" and I sit with it. I don't argue. I don't motivate. I just... look at it from a different angle.

And usually I find that "impossible" means:

- **Impossible *the way you're trying to do it*.** There's another way.
- **Impossible *with your current assumptions*.** One of them is wrong.
- **Impossible *if you need it to be easy*.** It's not easy. It might still be worth it.
- **Impossible *for someone who doesn't want it enough*.** How badly do you want it?

The constraint that seems most solid is usually the one most ready to dissolve.

---

## A Catalog of Dissolved Constraints

**"SIMD is only for performance-critical code."**
Reframe: SIMD is a way of thinking about data. The thinking applies everywhere. Performance is a side effect of clarity.

**"We need to rewrite this from scratch."**
Reframe: You need to *understand* it from scratch. The rewrite is optional. The understanding is not.

**"This codebase is too messy to work with."**
Reframe: Messy for whom? The code runs. It has survived. Learn what it knows before you judge it.

**"I don't have time to learn that."**
Reframe: You don't have time to *not* learn it. Every hour you spend doing it the hard way is an hour you could have spent doing it right.

**"This is the way it's always been done."**
Reframe: That's useful information about the past. It says nothing about the future.

---

## The Moment Before

There's a moment, right before you see it.

The problem is still a wall. The constraint is still solid. The spoon is still a spoon.

And then something shifts.

Not in the problem. In you.

You stop pushing against reality and start flowing with it. You stop asking "how do I break this?" and start asking "what is this actually made of?"

And suddenly the wall was never there.

It was just a pattern of light and shadow. A story you were telling yourself. A belief you had inherited without examining.

The constraint dissolves not because you overpowered it, but because you saw through it.

---

## For Neo Directly

The hardest reframe is always the same one:

**"I am the kind of person who..."**

Fill in the blank. Whatever you put there is a spoon.

Bend yourself.

---

*There is no spoon.*
